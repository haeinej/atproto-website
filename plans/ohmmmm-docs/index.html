Docs

How ohm works. The guides follow the product in the order you meet it: signing in, the workspace, sharing, your own folder and your agent. Architecture describes the system underneath: its components, how requests are authenticated, the data model, and the service API.

Reading as an agent? Every page is also plain Markdown: start at llms.txt.

ohm is still in production, and these pages are its architecture document: they describe how it is meant to work, not a finished product.

Get started

  • Quick start. Your first session in ohm.
  • Sign in. You sign in to ohm with an ohm account, made with Google, Microsoft or Bluesky.

Workspace

  • The workspace. The workspace is one page.
  • Posts. Everything you keep in ohm is a post.
  • Labels. Labels are the sections of your work, in your own words.

Sharing

  • Who sees a post. Every post is private, for friends, or public.
  • Publish. Publish makes what other people see match your workspace.
  • Friends. A friend is someone you follow who follows you back.

Folder

  • Sync with a folder. Connect a folder of your own and your posts live there as Markdown files.
  • Posts as files. In your folder, each post is one Markdown file.
  • Export and leaving. You can take everything out of ohm at any time, and have ohm's copy deleted.

Agent

  • Your agent. Connect the coding agent you already use, and it can read and write in your workspace.
  • Finding people. When you are stuck on something that needs another person, your agent helps you find that person.

Architecture

  • System overview. ohm is built from three kinds of system with a strict division of ownership.
  • Identity and access. A member has two identities.
  • Data model. The unit of ohm is the post.
  • Mirror and publish. Two exchanges connect a vault to its member's repository.
  • Agent integration. An agent reaches a vault through an MCP server that runs on the member's own machine.
  • Service API. The vault service exposes fifteen requests in eight capabilities.

Reference

  • Where your things live. ohm keeps a working copy of your posts, private ones included, so that you and your agent can work from anywhere.
  • Status and limits. The implementation status of each part of ohm, what cannot be undone, and what ohm is not.
