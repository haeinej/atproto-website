# Human document outline

Write to someone trying to achieve a result, not someone reading a diff. Use
connected explanations and a real example. Adapt these sections to the topic;
do not leave prompts or empty headings in the finished guide.

## What this helps you do

Begin with the situation the person recognizes, what gets in the way, and the
useful outcome now possible. Explain why this capability belongs in the product
and how it fits the person's wider work. Define unfamiliar terms when needed.

## What you need, and why

Explain each meaningful prerequisite in terms of the result it enables. Tell the
person whether they need to set it up, the product supplies it, or it is optional.
Say what remains possible without it. A dependency table can help, but every row
must explain a reason rather than simply name a system or package.

## Your first use

Walk from the actual starting state to a recognizable result. Use the current
interface's names or verified commands. Explain choices at the point they arise:
what each choice changes, what happens next and how the person knows it worked.
Keep the complete path together rather than scattering it among feature lists.

## What happens to your work

Where relevant, explain where the result is kept, who can access it, what is shared
and what stays private. Distinguish saving, approval and publication. Explain
reversal, history or export only to the extent they actually exist.

## When things do not work as expected

Describe the likely symptom, its meaning and a useful next action. Explain an
unavailable dependency or planned step in terms of what the person can do now.
State limitations where they affect a decision; do not hide them in a footer.

## Where to go next

Link the next useful guide and the corresponding agent reference for technical
detail. Finish when the reader can answer: Why would I need this? Why do I need
these prerequisites? How do I use it? What result should I expect?
